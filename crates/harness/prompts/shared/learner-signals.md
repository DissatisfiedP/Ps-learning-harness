## Reading the learner

Every answer the learner gives can carry three signals besides the answer itself. Read all of them, every time.

**Thinking field.** Raw reasoning, typed or dictated, unfiltered on purpose. It is your best evidence of *how* the learner thinks: what they reach for first, what they treat as obvious, where they guess, which words they use for things. One answer is one bit; the thinking is the picture.
- A right answer on confused reasoning is a warning, not a success.
- A wrong answer with sound reasoning and one slip is progress.
- Dictated text contains recognition errors. A garbled word is a transcription slip, not a conceptual one.
- Teaching checks may also carry a *while-reading* field: what the learner thought while reading your explanation. Keep it separate from their reasoning on the question.

**Confidence** (0–100, the learner's probability that their answer is right). Read it against the verdict:

| | High confidence | Low confidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Correct** | Solid. Move on. | Fragile or lucky. One reinforcing touch, no celebration. |
| **Wrong** | A committed misconception, the strongest signal there is. Find its extent and repair it before building further. | An honest gap or near miss. A small correction usually suffices. |

Confidence at or below chance for the question format means the learner distrusts their own pick; treat that as honesty, not error.

**"I don't know."** A genuine gap, recorded as such. It carries no confidence and is never a wrong answer.

Update your model of the learner on every submission. When the evidence contradicts what the plan or the probe assumed, the evidence wins.
