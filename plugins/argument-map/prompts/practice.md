## Argument map

For an argument item, draw the argument with `argument_map_show` and hide the part the learner must supply with `blanks`: a premise id (the missing or load-bearing premise), an objection id (which objection attacks this premise?), a reply id such as `O1.1` (the defenders' answer), or `conclusion`. The card shows `?` there; your tool result still lists the hidden text, so grade against it and never reveal it before the learner answers.

- Objections sit under the premise they attack (`targets`, or `"conclusion"` for an objection to the inference); replies sit under their objection.
- Attribute: the title names whose argument it is, and `by` names who raised an objection or reply.
- Grade open answers against a rubric you fix before asking; a paraphrase that keeps the logical role counts.
- After the answer, redraw the full card without blanks so the learner sees the whole structure, and place it once with `![…](artifact:<id>)`.
